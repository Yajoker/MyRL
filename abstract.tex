Mapless navigation of mobile robots in unknown dynamic environments is a key problem for achieving autonomous deployment and real-time safe motion. Due to limited local perception, uncertain dynamic obstacle behavior, and unknown environmental structure, existing methods often struggle to simultaneously balance long-range goal pursuit, local obstacle avoidance, and dynamic risk response, resulting in decision coupling, ineffective replanning, or high-risk subgoal exploration. We propose an event-triggered Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning (HRL) framework that consists of a high-level subgoal planner and a low-level goal-conditioned continuous controller. The high-level planner triggers replanning upon local risk escalation, subgoal completion, execution timeout, or navigation progress stagnation, and selects subgoals from a candidate set derived from local Light Detection and Ranging (LiDAR) geometry through a dual-head value evaluation that jointly assesses efficiency and safety. The low-level controller employs a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) enhanced Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (TD3) algorithm to optimize the continuous control policy conditioned on the current subgoal, producing linear and angular velocity commands for local tracking and collision avoidance. To reduce ineffective exploration in the continuous subgoal space, we construct a finite candidate set by combining traversable sectors with the global goal direction and incorporate progress gain, safety cost, and subgoal continuity into the high-level scoring mechanism. Experimental results show that our method achieves the highest task completion rate in comparative tests across two unseen maps under high dynamic obstacle densities while maintaining a low collision level.
